Research & Terminology

📚 Medical Terminology

Arrhythmia

An abnormal heartbeat where the heart beats too fast (tachycardia), too slow (bradycardia), or irregularly. Can range from harmless to life-threatening.

Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)

Occurs when blood flow to the heart muscle is blocked, causing tissue damage. A leading cause of death globally.

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

When blood pressure is consistently high, forcing the heart to work harder. A major risk factor for heart disease.

Cardiomyopathy

Disease of the heart muscle that makes it harder for the heart to pump blood to the rest of the body.

Angina Pectoris

Chest pain or discomfort caused by reduced blood flow to the heart. Often a warning sign of heart disease.

Atherosclerosis

Buildup of plaque in arteries that hardens and narrows them, reducing blood flow to the heart and other organs.

Heart Failure

When the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. Can be acute or chronic.

Fibrillation

Rapid, irregular heart contractions. Atrial fibrillation (AFib) is the most common type, affecting millions worldwide.

Cardiac Biomarkers

Proteins and enzymes released into the blood when the heart is damaged. Used to diagnose heart attacks and heart disease.

Ventricular Hypertrophy

Thickening of the heart's ventricle walls, usually due to high blood pressure or intense exercise.

🧬 CRISPR Genetic Editing Research

What is CRISPR?

CRISPR-Cas9 is a revolutionary gene-editing technology that allows scientists to precisely modify DNA sequences. It originated from a natural defense system found in bacteria.

  • CRISPR: Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats
  • Cas9: The enzyme that cuts DNA
  • Function: Acts like molecular scissors to edit genes

How CRISPR Works - Step by Step

  • 1. Design: Scientists design a guide RNA that matches the DNA sequence they want to edit
  • 2. Transport: The guide RNA and Cas9 enzyme are introduced into cells
  • 3. Recognition: The guide RNA directs Cas9 to the correct DNA location
  • 4. Cutting: Cas9 cuts both strands of the DNA at the target site
  • 5. Repair: The cell's natural repair mechanisms fix the cut, either deleting the gene or inserting new DNA

Advantages of CRISPR

  • ✓ Highly precise and accurate
  • ✓ Fast compared to previous gene-editing techniques
  • ✓ Cost-effective
  • ✓ Easier to use than older technologies
  • ✓ Can target multiple genes simultaneously

CRISPR Applications in Cardiac Medicine

  • Genetic Heart Diseases: Treating hereditary cardiomyopathies like Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)
  • Arrhythmia Treatment: Correcting genetic mutations that cause fatal arrhythmias
  • Heart Regeneration: Reprogramming cells to regenerate damaged heart tissue
  • Disease Modeling: Creating disease models to test new drugs
  • Preventive Medicine: Identifying and correcting disease-causing mutations before symptoms appear

Recent CRISPR Breakthroughs (2024-2026)

  • ✓ First CRISPR clinical trials approved for inherited retinal diseases
  • ✓ Development of improved CRISPR variants with higher accuracy
  • ✓ Progress in ex vivo cardiac cell therapy using CRISPR
  • ✓ New delivery methods for CRISPR to reach heart tissue

Ethical Considerations

  • Germline Editing: Debate over editing genes that can be passed to future generations
  • Off-target Effects: CRISPR may cut DNA at unintended locations
  • Accessibility: Cost and availability concerns for patients in developing countries
  • Unknown Consequences: Long-term effects of gene editing are still being studied
  • Consent & Privacy: Issues around genetic data and informed consent

Future Prospects for CRISPR in Cardiology

  • 🔮 Development of more precise CRISPR variants (CRISPR 3.0)
  • 🔮 In vivo gene therapy for direct heart tissue editing
  • 🔮 Combination therapies with stem cells and CRISPR
  • 🔮 Personalized medicine tailored to individual genetic profiles
  • 🔮 Potential cure for previously untreatable genetic heart diseases

❤️ Heart Anatomy & Physiology

Basic Structure

The human heart is a muscular organ about the size of a closed fist. It has four chambers: two upper atria and two lower ventricles.

Blood Flow Through the Heart

  1. Deoxygenated blood returns from the body to the right atrium
  2. Blood flows to the right ventricle
  3. Right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs
  4. Oxygenated blood returns from the lungs to the left atrium
  5. Blood flows to the left ventricle
  6. Left ventricle pumps blood to the entire body

Heart Rate & Cardiac Output

  • Resting Heart Rate: 60-100 beats per minute in adults
  • Cardiac Output: Amount of blood pumped per minute (typically 5L/min at rest)
  • Stroke Volume: Amount of blood pumped with each beat
  • Heart Rate × Stroke Volume = Cardiac Output
